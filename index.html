Overview

Abstract

Learning-based physical simulators produce explicit object dynamics, but their physical repertoire is typically bounded by the task-specific trajectories seen during training. Foundation vision-language models offer broader semantic and qualitative physical priors, yet are not trained to predict temporally dense, metrically calibrated state transitions.

We introduce a trajectory-grounded VLM for physical simulation. The model first predicts an object-centric physical event trace and sparse numerical translation waypoints. A shape-aware flow-matching decoder then conditions on this explicit plan and the initial object geometry to generate dense per-object SE(3) trajectories. Together with MOVi, our Rigid+ benchmark evaluates generalization to unseen shapes, object counts, physical parameters, and compositions of physical phenomena.

Method

From observations to dense dynamics

The explicit sparse trajectory is an interpretable interface between high-level physical reasoning and low-level geometric simulation.

The VLM predicts physical event trace R and sparse plan P. The decoder models p(X | P, S, A), using geometry, scale, and optional physical conditions to recover a dense horizon of rigid poses.

This decomposition avoids long autoregressive coordinate sequences while keeping the motion plan inspectable and independently editable.
